What this instrument is

This is the third of six papers in the 2022 Geography Teacher symposium on "GeoActivity Types in APHG." It documents four real field studies designed and run by two APHG teachers for ninth-graders, organized around Rice & Bulman's (2001) idea that fieldwork moves through three progressive approaches. The paper's contribution is a design comparator (Table 1) and a reflection-essay rubric (Table 2) — both reproduced faithfully below.

→

Observation

Teacher-led. Looking, seeing, and listening through guided tours or field demonstrations.

→

Investigation

More student-directed. Examination and measurement of a chosen site.

Inquiry

Most student-directed. Open-ended questions, hypothesis testing, a culminating product.

Rice & Bulman (2001), after Bland et al. (1996). The four designs below deliberately span this progression — two Observation, one Investigation, one Inquiry.

Reflection-essay rubric grader

After the Visiting Religious Communities field study, students write a 350–500-word reflection essay, graded on Table 2's rubric: two criteria — Reaction/Response and Completion — each rated on five levels. Pick a sample essay, choose the level you'd award for each criterion, then reveal how the rubric's indicators point to a grade.

Criterion A · Reaction / Response

Depth of thinking, organization, use of reasoning moves (compare/contrast, analogy, counterfactual, counterargument, cause/effect, connections), and use of evidence from readings, lecture, discussion, and the field study.

Criterion B · Completion

Whether the student included field-study photos and clearly covered every part of the assignment.

An honest note on scoring

Table 2 is a qualitative rubric — the five levels (Full marks → Not yet) carry descriptive indicators but the paper assigns them no point values. The optional 1–5 tally shown after you grade is added here only as a rough gauge; it is not part of the published instrument.

What's faithful, and what's illustrative

Sample essays are original illustrations

The paper does not publish the students' actual reflection essays, and Figures 1–2 (a student observation note and a store-layout sketch) are student work that isn't reproduced here. The three essays in the grader are original samples written for this page to exercise the rubric — they are not from the study. The rubric criteria, levels, and indicator wording, and all four Table 1 design attributes, are reproduced faithfully from the article.

Scope of the evidence

These are four field studies from two APHG teachers, offered as transferable examples rather than an evaluated intervention. The authors themselves note field study "might not directly help students prepare for the content on the APHG exam," locating its value instead in first-hand experience, geographic memory, and skills. No effect sizes or student-outcome statistics are reported in this paper.
