Two routines that turn “glancing at a map” into analyzing it

Textbooks are full of maps and photos students barely read. These two student-facing scaffolds — OPTIC for photos and SCRAP for maps — give learners a fixed set of steps to unpack a visual and write about it. Try each below: pick a step, read the prompt, and write your own analysis.

Jo, I., Crane, M., Hong, J. E. & Huh, S. (2022). GeoActivity Types in APHG: Analysis of Maps and Photos. The Geography Teacher 19(2):56–59.

The point: practice writing, the “four F’s”

Both routines are daily warm-ups built to grow four writing habits (Walker 1996). Students go from a few words in September to a solid paragraph by spring.

F

Fluency

the ability to write extensively.

F

Frequency

the habit of writing often.

F

Flexibility

producing diverse writing products.

F

Formality

using language rules and structure.

Grade by completion. No penalty for a “wrong” answer — reassure students it’s OK to be wrong. Confidence first.
Feedback, varied. Don’t mark every step every day — focus one day on strugglers, another on oral feedback in discussion, another on just one OPTIC/SCRAP component.
Start light. First time, just hand out the tool and a visual and see what students do. Discuss one right and one wrong answer.
Use real sources. National Geographic photos, Menzel’s books, Esri atlases — and the teacher’s own travel/fieldwork photos work best of all.
Part of a series. This is one of the 2022 “GeoActivity Types in APHG” articles in The Geography Teacher. Its siblings cover discussions, field study (with field-note templates), exam preparation, and an end-of-year culminating project — a whole toolkit of student-facing routines. SCRAP is adapted from Gersmehl’s (2014) modes of spatial thinking; OPTIC comes from the Texas Education Agency’s Lighthouse Project (2006).
