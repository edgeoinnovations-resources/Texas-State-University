What this is

A reflective study of one course — Active Learning in Geography (GEO5340), a five-week, fully online summer graduate course at Texas State (Canvas LMS, Summer 2020) in a master's program for educators. Its capstone: each student builds an Active Learning Unit (ALU) for their own classroom. To make sense of what worked, the authors cross two established frameworks.

The rows · Moore (1989)

Three types of online interaction

  1. Learner–content — readings, videos, quizzes, and the work you produce.
  2. Learner–instructor — meetings, office hours, and feedback.
  3. Learner–learner — group meetings, discussion boards, and peer review.
The columns · Sulla (2019)

Three goals of active learning

  1. Engagement — applying knowledge to solve a real problem, not busywork.
  2. Responsibility — students take charge of their learning, not just comply.
  3. Academic rigor — authentic tasks that demand deep, rigorous work.

What the authors recommend

The paper closes with practical recommendations — for students who want to learn actively online, and for instructors who want to build the environment for it.

For active online learners

Take charge of your learning

  • Commit to all course activities, and understand the premises of active learning.
  • Keep regular interactions with the instructor and peers to build rapport and community.
  • Take responsibility beyond attending — stay organized, plan and monitor progress, and actively seek feedback.
  • Learn the institutional resources available (library services, tech support, software licenses).
For instructors

Design for active engagement

  • Connect projects and major tasks to students' personal goals — relevance drives immersion.
  • Allow flexibility and choice while keeping the rigor — that evokes a sense of ownership.
  • Maintain instructor presence across private and public channels; use intro videos and low-stakes synchronous small-group tasks.
  • Don't let technology become a barrier — be ready to point students to technical help.

Honesty notes

The 3 × 3 is a faithful synthesis, not a printed table

The paper is organized by Sulla's three goals (its main sections are Engagement, Responsibility, and Academic rigor) and it uses Moore's three interaction types as "consistent language" throughout — but it does not print a 3 × 3 matrix. Crossing them into this grid is a way to organize the paper's own content. Every testimonial in the cells is a verbatim quote from the article, attributed to the student who wrote it.

A reflective account, not an experiment

This is a testimonial paper: nine students took the course, and three co-authored it, reflecting alongside the instructor. The authors are explicit — "these reflections and testimonials are personal, and we have no intention to generalize our findings." It was also a Summer-2020 course, so the online setting was shaped by the pandemic. Both frameworks are borrowed — Moore's (1989) interaction types and Sulla's (2019) learner-active goals — which is why this page is marked reused (blue). The value is a rich, first-person map of how interactions serve active-learning goals.
