Interactive Instrument · Jo & Bednarz (2014) ★

Are you inclined to teach spatial thinking?

This inventory doesn’t test what a teacher knows — it measures whether they’re disposed to actually do it. Answer the items below and watch your five-dimension disposition profile build in real time, and see the clever reverse-scoring safeguard catch inconsistent responses.

Jo, I. & Bednarz, S. W. (2014). Dispositions Toward Teaching Spatial Thinking Through Geography. Journal of Geography 113(5):198–207.

Ability / knowledge

A teacher can explain distribution, run a GIS overlay, design a spatial question. Necessary — but it doesn’t guarantee they’ll ever choose to.

≠

Disposition

The inclination, belief, and willingness to actually reach for those skills in the classroom. That “will they bother?” tendency is what this instrument measures.

Five dimensions of the disposition

The 40-item inventory is built from five belief-and-inclination subcategories. Each maps to an axis on the profile you’ll build below.

Take a slice of the inventory

The validity safeguard, up close

Five of the 40 items are “counter-statements” — agreeing with them signals less inclination, so they’re scored backwards. It’s a quiet trap for people who just click “agree” down the page. Try it:

#16 (reverse-scored): “I believe that geography is best learned by rote memorization of facts.”

Why it matters. A respondent who “strongly agrees” with rote memorization (a 5) is telling you they’re not disposed toward inquiry-driven spatial thinking — so it counts as a 1. Reverse items keep an all-“agree” response from faking a high score, and let researchers flag people whose answers contradict each other.

The life of the instrument

Proposed in 2014 as a conceptual exemplar — then picked up and put to work, growing from a classroom of 14 to over a thousand teachers across two nations.
