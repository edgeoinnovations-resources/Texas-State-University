What this instrument is

Discussion is one of the "GeoActivity" types the symposium documents. This paper focuses on the Socratic seminar — a whole-class method where students examine an issue from many perspectives to build deeper understanding. The paper's toolkit is three things: a bank of question types to fuel dialogue (Table 1), a set of unit topics to discuss (Table 2), and a way to assess it (rubric, Fig. 2). The recommendation: at least one Socratic seminar per unit, at the end of the unit, since it serves as a cumulative review.

Then students flip roles and discuss again.

The fishbowl setup

Students split into participants (inner circle, actively discussing) and observers (outer circle). Each observer is assigned one participant to watch, tracking engagement and conversation on a peer-evaluation form — then the two groups swap. Assigning the observer role keeps non-speaking students engaged and generates constructive feedback.

Discussion rubric

A quantitative tool like Equity Maps can chart who spoke and for how long, but it only captures quantity. To judge quality too, the paper recommends an analytic rubric spanning six dimensions. Score a hypothetical student on each to see how an analytic rubric resolves.

What's faithful here

The six dimensions are exactly the ones the paper names for a discussion rubric — levels of participation, content, evidence used to support an argument, critical and analytical thinking, discussion skills, and appropriate language use. The paper's actual rubric (Fig. 2) is an image sourced from Argo Community High School and is not reproduced in the article's text, so the level descriptors and the 1–4 scoring below are original illustrations built to exercise those dimensions — not the Argo rubric itself.

5

Honesty notes

Adapted, not original, question stems

The eight question types and their stems are reproduced faithfully from Table 1, which the authors themselves adapted from AVID Socratic Seminar (2014), National Geographic's Geographic Thinking (2017), and Facing History & Ourselves' Socratic Seminar Stems (2022). The unit topics and example questions in the builder are verbatim from Table 2.

A practice paper, not an evaluated study

This is a practitioner article sharing classroom methods from APHG teachers. It reports no experimental design, sample, or outcome statistics — its evidence is professional recommendation and teacher testimony ("overwhelmingly positive reactions to discussions"). Figures 1 (Equity Maps output) and 2 (the rubric) are example images and are described, not reproduced.
