Energy Profile Quadrant Sorter

Why is energy a different problem for Saudi Arabia than for Japan, or for Nigeria than for Germany? This inquiry lesson has students map real energy data, then sort countries by how much energy they use and how much they import — revealing why each group faces a different energy-policy priority.

What this is

A 45-minute high-school lesson (world geography or AP Human Geography) built around two issues: fossil-fuel dependency and growing energy demand. Students use ArcGIS Online web maps to examine where energy is produced, consumed, imported, and accessed — aligned to C3 Framework geography standards. It follows the classic geographic-inquiry arc, and the reasoning payoff is the use × import matrix you'll try below.

Ask

Frame the questions

Does the world have enough energy? What are the major energy issues?

Acquire

Map the data

Import per-capita energy-use CSVs; add import, production & access layers.

Explore

Read the patterns

Fossil-fuel reliance, oil producers vs. consumers, electricity access.

Analyze

Predict trends

Will demand grow? Does it differ by region?

Answer

Weigh policy

Implications of demand growth, fossil fuels, and CO₂.

Sort countries by energy use × import

Handout 1 asks: where would you put Saudi Arabia? Norway, Canada, Russia? The US, Germany, France? Click a country, then click the quadrant you'd place it in — energy use per capita across the top, net energy import down the side. Then reveal each quadrant's real energy-policy priority. (A negative import means a country is a net exporter.)

Honesty notes

The 2 × 2 is the lesson's; the answer key is derived

The use × import matrix, the countries named for it, the producer/consumer lists, and the access figures are all straight from the lesson. But the lesson has students derive each country's quadrant from the maps — it doesn't print a fixed answer key. The placements checked here reflect real energy data and the lesson's own framing (exporters like Saudi Arabia and Norway have negative net imports; the US, Germany, and France are high-use importers; Nigeria is the headline "exports oil yet lacks power" case). The exact High-30 / Low-30 per-capita lists live in the lesson's downloadable World Bank CSVs, not the article text.

A snapshot on ~2013 data

The lesson's figures reflect roughly 2013 World Bank / energy data (with 2003 and 2008 also in the files), so specific rankings will have shifted since — the US has swung from big importer toward net exporter, for example. Treat this as a teaching model of the logic of energy geography, not a current scoreboard. The lesson's own arc ends by weighing continued fossil-fuel reliance and CO₂ growth against a shift to renewables.
