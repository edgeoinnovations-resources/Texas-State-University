The trap, in one chart

Plot each location by how many questions it holds (across) against how spatial those questions are (up). The relationship runs backwards: the locations packed with questions are the least spatial, and the richest location holds the fewest questions.

The sharpest split: do questions even use a map?

Using a representation (map, diagram, graph) is one of the three components. The gap between where the rich questions live and where teachers look is almost total — 98% vs. 8%.

The marginalized margins. Over 90% of “Section Assessment” questions have nothing to do with using a spatial representation — yet these, plus chapter assessments, are exactly the questions teachers lean on because their titles promise to “cover everything.” The genuinely spatial questions — ~98% using a representation, ~75% integrating all three components — are the “Skill Builder” prompts in the margins that get treated as optional extras.

What the study tells teachers to do

1

Mine the margins. Use the page-margin “Skill Builder” questions frequently — don’t treat them as filler.

2

Pick questions with a representation. Favor questions built around a map, diagram, or graph.

3

Push the cognition higher. Choose questions that ask not just “where,” but “why there.”
