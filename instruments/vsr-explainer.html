Method, animated · Huh & Jo (2024)

How do you study a decision a teacher never said out loud?

Most of teaching happens in a teacher’s head — split-second choices to drop a slide, shift scale, or dodge a controversy. This is how the 2024 pedagogical-reasoning study made that invisible reasoning visible: it filmed the lesson, then played the footage back to the teacher and asked, “what were you thinking right there?” Below, you can watch the method work.

Huh, S. & Jo, I. (2024). Geography Teachers’ Pedagogical Reasoning and Action While Integrating Sustainable Development into APHG Courses. Journal of Geography 123(4):97–111.

Where the recall lands: Shulman’s reasoning cycle

The study is built on Shulman’s six-phase Pedagogical Reasoning & Action cycle — a recursive loop, not a straight line. Each data source captures a different phase. Click a phase to see what captured it; the two gold phases are exactly what video-stimulated recall reaches.

Click a phase →

Shulman’s model runs comprehension → transformation → instruction → evaluation → reflection → new comprehension, then loops. The authors mapped each phase to a specific data source.

Why it mattersVideo-stimulated recall is the only source that reaches Reflection and New Comprehension — the phases where teachers reconsider what they did and rebuild their understanding.

The interview protocol

A single semi-structured, post-lesson interview walked each teacher back through all six phases — questions adapted from Peterson & Treagust (1995) and Nguyen et al. (2013). The Reflection questions are the ones asked while the clip is playing. Click to open each phase.

What the method surfaced

Reading the recordings against the plans let the researchers separate two kinds of reasoning — and the recall interviews explained both.

Planned pedagogical reasoning

Deliberate, pre-thought choices:

  • Placing the sustainability lesson in Unit 1, before exam pressure
  • Scale-shifting to build relational thinking (school → bay → ocean)
  • Local, authentic data — students’ own litter, real flood maps
  • Tailoring to students’ backgrounds and prior knowledge

Reasoning under pressure (the improvised moves)

Wisdom-of-practice, decided mid-lesson:

  • Neutralizing political controversy on the fly
  • Flipping a lecture to homework when discussion overran
  • Working around a ban on the words “climate change”
  • Abandoning an action project blocked by administration

The hardest thing to reach, the study found, was the last step of real sustainability education — moving from teaching about it to letting students act on it. Time, curriculum crowding, and institutional resistance kept getting in the way. Without video-stimulated recall, those quiet in-the-moment decisions — and the reasons behind them — would have stayed invisible.
