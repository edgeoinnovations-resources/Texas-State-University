What does “ready for college geography” actually mean — and are students there?

This study reverse-engineered college readiness for geography: cross-walking three standards documents into seven goals, then surveying 353 educators on how important each goal is and how proficient students are. The result is a stark “expectations gap” you can explore below.

Who prioritizes what — the expectations mismatch

The study’s core finding wasn’t just low proficiency — it was that professors and teachers value different goals. Here’s the difference in importance ratings (professors − teachers) for each goal. ★ marks a statistically significant gap.

◀ Teachers value moreProfessors value more ▶
The gap that matters most: professors rated Goals 5 & 6 — the higher-level spatial-thinking goals — significantly more important, while teachers rated Goals 2, 3 & 4 higher. The authors read this as teachers placing lower priority on the very spatial-thinking skills college courses assume — a gap they trace to how little geography teacher-prep requires.
